Who made the Cub Hub?

Meet Team Wolf.

Team Wolf is a pack of real scientists, advocates, and wolf lovers working to help wolves thrive all across America. Wolves have gotten a bad rap in stories for way too long. We're here with the real facts, and a mission: making sure wolves have a home in the wild for good.

Wolves keep nature in balance

When wolves are around, rivers, forests, and other animals do better too.

We check the facts

We make sure big decisions about wolves are based on real science.

We speak up

Wolves can't write letters. You can. Every voice counts.

The Rendezvous.

When wolf pups are big enough to leave their den, the pack moves them to a rendezvous site. It’s a safe, open meadow where the pups play, explore, and learn from the grown-ups while the rest of the pack goes hunting.

    Fact card 1Where did your dog come from?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 1Every dog on Earth, from Chihuahuas to Great Danes, comes from ancient wolves.
    Fact card 2Are Gray Wolves always gray?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 2Nope! Gray Wolves can be black, white, tan, or a mix.
    Fact card 3How strong is a wolf’s nose?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 3About 100 times stronger than yours. A wolf’s nose has about 200 million scent sensors. Yours has about 5 million!
    Fact card 4Myth or fact: a wolf pack is a gang of strangers.Tap to flip ↻Fact card 4Myth! A pack is a family: usually a mom, a dad, and their kids. Most packs have 7 or 8 wolves.
    Fact card 5How big is a newborn wolf pup?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 5About 1 pound, lighter than a can of soda. Newborn pups can’t see or hear yet.
    Fact card 6Why do wolf pups play so much?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 6Tag, hide-and-seek, and wrestling teach pups how to hunt and get along.
    Fact card 7Myth or fact: wolves howl at the moon.Tap to flip ↻Fact card 7Myth! But wolves do howl more when the moon is full and bright.
    Fact card 8How far away can you hear a howl?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 8Up to 10 miles in open country. Scientists can even tell wolves apart by their howls!
    Fact card 9Why do wolves howl?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 9To find each other when the pack is spread out, and to tell other packs, “This is our home. Stay away.”
    Fact card 10What do Yellowstone’s wolves eat most?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 10Elk! They also eat deer, moose, bison, and beavers.
    Fact card 11Who are the “wolf-birds”?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 11Ravens! They show up at wolf kills within minutes to share the leftovers. Scientists found they remember where wolves hunt most.
    Fact card 12Myth or fact: wolves cause most livestock losses.Tap to flip ↻Fact card 12Myth! Wolves cause less than 1% of livestock losses. Weather, sickness, and birthing trouble cause far more.
    Fact card 13When did wolves come back to Yellowstone?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 13In 1995, after being gone for about 70 years.
    Fact card 14What’s a keystone species?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 14Like the keystone in a bridge, take it out and the whole ecosystem can fall out of balance. Wolves are a keystone species.
    Fact card 15What happened when wolves came home to Yellowstone?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 15Trees grew back along the rivers, beavers and songbirds returned, and the rivers changed.
    Fact card 16Myth or fact: real wolves are big and bad.Tap to flip ↻Fact card 16Myth! Real wolves are shy and usually stay far away from people.
    Fact card 17Which city has a wolf in its oldest story?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 17Rome! In an old Roman story, a she-wolf cared for two baby brothers, Romulus and Remus.
    Fact card 18What do the Ojibwe people call the wolf?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 18Ma’iingan. They teach that what happens to wolves happens to people too.
    Fact card 19How many Gray Wolves live in the lower 48 states?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 19About 8,000, in ten states.
    Fact card 20How much of their old home do wolves still live on?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 20Only about 10% of the land they used to call home.
    Fact card 21What protects cows and sheep best?Tap to flip ↻Fact card 21Guard dogs, fences, and range riders are more than twice as good at protecting cows and sheep as killing wolves.

        Dogs and pets.

        For grown-ups: dogs and traps

        In Montana, legal trapping can happen on public land from November into April, depending on the animal. Montana Fish, Wildlife & Parks says most dogs caught in traps were running off-leash or out of sight. Its advice: keep your dog on a leash or always in sight, and learn how to open a trap before you need to.

        About the Cub Hub

        Real facts. Made for kids.

        Team Wolf is a pack of advocates, scientists, and conservationists on a mission to ensure the long-term protection and recovery of Gray Wolves. We built the Cub Hub for kids ages 8 to 12: everything here is checked against the science and written so a kid can follow it.

        Classrooms, camps, and after-school clubs are welcome. Use any page, print any coloring sheet, and run the quiz with your group.

        Age level

        Made for ages 8 to 12, about grades 3 to 7. Lessons use short sentences and real facts. Younger kids do great with a grown-up reading along. See the reading list by age and the teacher’s guide.

        Your kid's privacy
        • The Cub Hub never asks kids for an email, phone number, or address.
        • The pack role game runs on the page. Names aren't saved or sent anywhere.
        • The governor letter is something your kid writes and mails, with your help.
        • We count visits with Google Analytics to see which pages kids use, so we can make the Cub Hub better. That's all it's used for: no ads, no profiles, nothing that identifies your kid.

        Want to do more?

        The grown-up version of the Cub Hub lives at teamwolf.org: the latest on wolves and the actions that matter right now.

        For teachers and club leaders

        Teacher’s guide to the Rendezvous.

        The Rendezvous is seven short lessons on how wolves live, built for kids ages 8 to 12. Each one works on a projector with the whole class or on one device per kid.

        7

        lessons, about 10 minutes each. Short facts, one hands-on activity, and one check question.

        8

        patches to earn: one per lesson, plus Wolf Expert for scoring 80% or better on the wolf trivia.

        2

        kinds of certificates. Each patch has its own, and all eight unlock the Official Pack Leader certificate. Print them or save a copy.

        0

        accounts or emails. Progress and names stay in that browser only. On a shared computer, kids share progress, so a whole-class run works best there.

        Running a lesson with your class

        1. Open the lesson on the big screen and read the facts out loud together.
        2. Do the activity as a group. Let kids take turns tapping.
        3. Ask the “Talk about it” question below before the check question.
        4. Answer the check question with a class vote. Getting it wrong is part of learning.
        5. Print the patch certificate for the class, or one per kid.

        Who is Team Wolf

        The pack behind the Cub Hub.

        Team Wolf is a coalition of wildlife advocates, outdoor people, educators, and scientists working to keep Gray Wolves a permanent part of the American wild. The Cub Hub is our program for kids.

        46partner organizations in the pack
        97,000+emails sent to decision-makers since 2022
        20 million+views of our wolf posts since 2022
        We speak up

        We push lawmakers and wildlife agencies to make wolf decisions based on science, in public.

        We tell the real story

        We swap the old big-bad-wolf story for the facts, through art, comics, films, and pages like this one.

        We bring people together

        Conservation groups, ranchers, hunters, teachers, and scientists who agree on one thing: wolves belong here.

        Team Wolf was co-founded by Leslie Kim Williams and Samantha Attwood. Our science lead is Dr. Peter Kareiva.
